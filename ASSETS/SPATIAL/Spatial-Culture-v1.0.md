Version: 1.0

Status: Active

Owner: HSDD

# Purpose

This document defines the Spatial Culture framework used within HSDD.

Spatial Culture is the practice of designing environments that influence behavior, communication, creativity, and experience.

Space is treated as an active system rather than a passive container.

---

# Definition

Spatial Culture is the relationship between:

- People
- Environment
- Behavior
- Narrative
- Experience

The objective is to create environments that support desired outcomes.

---

# Core Principle

People shape spaces.

Spaces shape people.

Both evolve together over time.

---

# Spatial Formula

```text
Space
    ↓
Behavior
    ↓
Experience
    ↓
Narrative
    ↓
Culture
```

A change in space can influence every layer above it.

---

# Core Principles

## 1. Space Is Communication

Every space communicates something.

Examples:

- Order or disorder
- Focus or distraction
- Openness or separation
- Creativity or routine

No space is neutral.

---

## 2. Space Should Support Purpose

A space should make the intended behavior easier.

Questions:

- What activity happens here?
- What behavior should be encouraged?
- What behavior should be reduced?

---

## 3. Experience Is Designed

Experiences are influenced by:

- Layout
- Lighting
- Objects
- Movement
- Visibility
- Sound

Culture emerges from repeated experiences.

---

## 4. Space Should Create Clarity

Good spaces reduce confusion.

People should understand:

- Where they are
- What happens here
- How the environment is intended to function

---

## 5. Space Is Part of the Brand

People remember environments.

Spaces become part of organizational identity.

---

# Spatial Components

## Environment

Physical and digital surroundings.

Examples:

- Rooms
- Workstations
- Websites
- Digital workspaces

---

## Objects

Tools and artifacts within a space.

Examples:

- Furniture
- Equipment
- Documents
- Displays

---

## Flow

How people move through a space.

Questions:

- What is visible?
- What is hidden?
- What receives attention?

---

## Interaction

How people engage with each other and with the environment.

# Spatial Design Questions

Before designing a space, ask:

1. What behavior should occur here?
2. What behavior should not occur here?
3. What experience should be remembered?
4. What narrative does this space communicate?
5. How does this support identity?

# Objective

The objective of Spatial Culture is not decoration.

The objective is alignment.

Create environments that align identity, behavior, experience, and culture.
